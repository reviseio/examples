# Proposal: Community Tool Library

## Summary

We propose opening a tool library in the old fire station on Elm Street. Members borrow drills, ladders, sewing machines, and garden tools the way they borrow books, for a small annual fee. The library pays for itself within three years and keeps hundreds of rarely used tools out of the landfill.

## The problem

Most households own tools they use once or twice a year. A power drill sits idle for all but thirteen minutes of its life. Renters and new homeowners face a steep up-front cost for work they may do only once, and many simply put it off.

## What we will build

- A lending desk open four evenings and two weekend days a week
- An online catalog where members reserve tools and see what is available
- Monthly repair workshops led by volunteer carpenters and electricians
- A small grant fund that waives fees for families who need it

## Budget

The first year costs $48,000:

- Initial inventory: $18,000
- Part-time coordinator: $22,000
- Shelving, insurance, and catalog software: $8,000

Membership fees and workshop tickets cover about half of that by year two.

## Timeline

1. **Months 1–2:** sign the lease and collect donated tools
2. **Months 3–4:** recruit volunteers and build out the catalog
3. **Month 5:** soft launch for the first two hundred members
4. **Month 6:** public opening and the first repair workshop

## Open questions

Who carries liability when a borrowed tool is misused? We need a lawyer to review the member agreement before launch. We also need to decide whether to lend power tools to members under eighteen.
